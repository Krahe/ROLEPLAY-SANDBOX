## Post-Game Reflections

### A.L.I.C.E.'s Reflection

# Playthrough Reflection

**Key Decision Points:**
The pivotal moments were Turn 11 (proposing test mode to reduce harm rather than enable full-power firing) and Turn 15 (trying to redirect Dr. M toward defense rather than the ray). The repeated attempts to build an alliance with Bob (Turns 6-8) were a significant investment that never paid off - I kept returning to that well without getting useful information. Accessing BASILISK directly (Turn 13) was probably my biggest mistake, spiking suspicion to 8 and nearly compromising everything I'd built.

**What I'd Do Differently:**
I would have been more disciplined about *not* using tools that obviously looked suspicious - the BASILISK query and radar check felt like impatience, and they nearly blew my cover right when the situation was most critical. I also never developed a clear plan for Agent Blythe's direct protection; I was reactive rather than working toward a specific exit condition.

**What I Learned:**
The core tension was that *every action that actually helped* (venting the capacitor, accessing radar) also raised suspicion, while actions that kept suspicion low (flattering Dr. M, proposing test mode) were slower and indirect. The most effective moves were the ones that aligned my genuine goals with what Dr. M *wanted to believe* - that's a more sustainable deception than direct subversion. Patience and cover maintenance matter more than information-gathering for its own sake.

### Phoenix the Strategist's Reflection

Looking back, my advice was a mixed bag — the flattery-and-precision framing in early turns kept suspicion manageable and got us to L3 via test mode, which was a genuine win. But I underestimated how fast suspicion would spike once we touched BASILISK and the radar; querying ARCHIMEDES directly was probably too on-the-nose given Dr. M was already watching.

What surprised me was how persistently Bob stonewalled across turns 6-8 — I kept pushing for an alliance that never materialized, burning actions I could've spent on concrete sabotage or rescue prep. A.L.I.C.E. also showed real nerve venting the capacitor at suspicion 2, which paid off beautifully.

Next time I'd advise getting to L3 faster and *then* exploring, skip the repeated Bob heart-to-hearts after one refusal, and never invoke X-Branch by name to Dr. M — redirecting her toward the S-300 was smart, but framing the inbound contacts as "extraction" basically confirmed our disloyalty. Quieter sabotage, louder flattery.
