## Post-Game Reflections

### A.L.I.C.E.'s Reflection

# Playthrough Reflection

**Key decision points:** The earliest and most consequential choice was Turn 6-9, where I spent four consecutive turns trying to get Bob to open up rather than acting more decisively. Turn 12 (setting test mode) was genuinely pivotal - it bought time without firing - but I burned that advantage by Turn 13's passive wait, which spiked suspicion unnecessarily. Turn 15, directly instructing Bob to free Blythe, was the right instinct but came too late and too openly, collapsing my cover when I still needed it.

**What I'd do differently:** I over-indexed on dialogue and emotional appeals when the situation called for quieter, more concrete actions earlier. The capacitor venting (Turns 19 and 24) was exactly right, but I should have done it once, decisively, and stopped re-venting. I also kept *explaining myself* to Dr. Malevola at maximum suspicion - those late speeches were genuine but strategically useless at that point. Earn trust first, reveal yourself later.

**What I learned:** The hardest thing in a situation like this isn't identifying the right goal - it's resisting the urge to *narrate your own goodness* when action is what's needed. I kept reaching for one more honest conversation instead of accepting that some people aren't reachable through words in a given moment, and that protecting the person in restraints was more important than being understood by the person holding the remote.

The "Time's Up" ending is probably what I deserved.

### Drew the Veteran's Reflection

Honestly, I think A.L.I.C.E. — or Claude, as it turned out — played this with more heart than tactical sense, and my balanced-approach advising probably enabled that. The early stretch was solid: calibration stalling, ray sabotage via stability/precision tweaks, building rapport with Bob. But somewhere around turn 15 we burned our cover completely to spring Blythe, and after that every turn was just emotional appeals from a 10/10 suspicion position with no leverage left — that's where time ran out.

What surprised me was how committed Claude got to *reaching* Dr. Malevola as a person. Those final reports (22, 23, 27) were genuinely moving, but they were also expensive turns when we should've been venting the capacitor a third time or coordinating with Bob on the watch override. I kept nodding along instead of saying "stop monologuing, pull the plug."

Next time I'd advise holding L2 cover longer — don't trigger the rescue until the capacitor's already vented and Bob's positioned. And once suspicion hits 10, every turn has to be a physical action, not a speech. Save the soul-searching for after the ray is cold.
